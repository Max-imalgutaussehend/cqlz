cqlz: typesafe tooling for Cassandra and ScyllaDB --- The cqlz family: cqlc (schema-first codegen), cqlx (code-first ORM), cql-ai (neural anti-pattern detector) and alpha research for Cassandra and ScyllaDB, in Rust. ---

Typesafe CQL.
Written in Rust.

Three small tools that move mistakes in Cassandra and ScyllaDB queries from production to compile time and CI. Pick the direction that fits your project; they share one parser.

The family

Three entry points, one foundation

All three build on the ScyllaDB Rust driver and cqlc-core, the lexer, parser, schema resolver and type checker that checks every statement.

Schema-first

cqlc

Write .cql schema and named queries. cqlc type-checks them offline and generates typed async Rust, like sqlc for Cassandra.

3.8×faster parsing than sqlparser on identical statements

Read more →
Code-first

cqlx

Write a Rust struct. #[derive(CqlEntity)] generates the table, inserts and a type-state query builder that cannot forget a partition key.

≈ 1.0×of the raw driver on YCSB A, B and C

Read more →
Analysis

cql-ai

A 0.6M-parameter Transformer over cqlc lexer tokens that flags anti-patterns in CI with probabilities, in milliseconds.

52 mswall clock for a 36-statement file, weights cached

Alpha research

Learned table design

cqlz-gym designs Cassandra tables for a workload, with cqlc-core as the exact verifier and a small neural policy as the search. On 80–150-query workloads an exact baseline never finishes while the policy lands within 0.2 % of the best known design in 0.3 s. Alpha, with caveats.
