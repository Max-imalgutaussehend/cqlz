cqlz-gym

Give it entities and a weighted query workload, get the Cassandra tables. An integer program and a small learned policy search for the design, cqlc-core verifies every candidate, and a calibrated cost model scores it.

How it works

Data modelling as a verified search

Cassandra data modelling means choosing denormalised tables so every query is a cheap single-partition read. The standard automated approach, NoSE (Mior, Salem, Aboulnaga, Liu, IEEE TKDE 2017), solves an integer program. cqlz-gym keeps that formulation as the exact baseline and adds a policy that builds the design step by step. After each step cqlc-core decides whether a query is valid and warning-free against the proposed schema; invalid actions are masked, so every proposed design is valid by construction and the reward needs no labelled data.

Real workloads

Designs for domain workloads

cqlz-gym validate loads the naive, greedy and best design into a real ScyllaDB (Docker, one node, 10,000 rows per entity, 32 clients, 6 s per design) and measures the workload mix. The three workloads are modelled on DataStax data-modelling examples (sensors, shopping) and KillrVideo.

The direction of the model is right and its gain is overstated (−23 % predicted, −7.6 % latency measured). One cqlc-core verification takes 3.1 µs, and the scaling efficiency is 96 % on two, 91 % on four and 67 % on eight threads (the last four cores of an M3 are efficiency cores). The environment is cheap enough that the search, not the checking, is the cost.

Limits

What to keep in mind

  • The model is simplified: one equality-restricted partition and at most one range column per query, independent entities, no secondary indexes, materialised views or LWT; at most 256 candidate tables.
  • The cost model is calibrated on measurements from the cql-ai dataset (seek, per-row and per-write cost), but it is still a model. A policy can exploit its errors, so check final designs with cqlz-gym validate on a real ScyllaDB.
  • Learned search matters at large workloads; at small sizes the exact solvers win or tie and the policy adds nothing.
  • Related work: neural combinatorial optimisation (Bello et al., 2016; Kool, van Hoof, Welling, ICLR 2019), Brax (Freeman et al., 2021) for making the environment the scalable part, and RL for NoSQL index selection and relational schemas. We found none for Cassandra schema design; that rests on a handful of searches, not a literature review.
